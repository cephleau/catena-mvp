The Denver/Colorado market is strong for interpretation services. The state has a large Spanish-speaking population, growing refugee communities (Somali, Amharic, Nepali, Burmese, Arabic), and robust federal/state legal activity. ## PART 1: COURT SYSTEMS

### 1A. Colorado State Courts (Colorado Judicial Branch)

**Who they are:** 22 judicial districts covering all state-level courts — district courts, county courts, juvenile courts, family courts. Denver is in the **2nd Judicial District**.

**How they hire interpreters:**
- The Judicial Branch does NOT use the Colorado State Procurement Code — they have their own Purchasing Fiscal Rules
- Competitive solicitations are posted on **Rocky Mountain BidNet** (their e-procurement system)
- Register free at: **bidnetdirect.com/officeofthestatecourtadministratorcoloradojudicial**
- Once registered, you'll be notified of interpreter-related solicitations

**Key contacts:**
- Purchasing Unit: **FSDPurchasing@judicial.state.co.us**
- Vendor Liaison (for service providers): **vendorsupport@judicial.state.co.us**
- Contracts Management Unit: handles drafting, compliance, and administration

**Criminal History Check (CHC) requirement:**
Every contractor providing services under a Judicial contract must clear a background check. You can use American Data Bank (ADB) — **cojudicialcompliance.com** — fee is $36.95/person. This applies to your interpreters, not just your company.

**Forum system:** Colorado Judicial uses a contract management portal called Forum (forum-colorado.ivalua.app). Vendors register and manage contracts here.

**Access to Justice Unit:** This unit within the Judicial Branch handles language access. They are the program owners for interpreter services — worth contacting directly once you're in the BidNet system.

**Realistic path:**
1. Register on BidNet now (free)
2. Email FSDPurchasing@ to introduce Catena and ask about upcoming interpreter service solicitations
3. Get your interpreter roster credentialed (CHCs completed)
4. Monitor BidNet for RFPs — interpreter contracts often go out by district

### 1B. US District Court — District of Colorado (Federal Court)

**Who they are:** The federal trial court for Colorado. Located at the Alfred A. Arraj United States Courthouse, 901 19th St, Denver, CO 80294.

**Website:** **www.cod.uscourts.gov**

**How federal court interpretation works:**
- Federal courts use three interpreter tiers:
  - **Federally Certified** (passed FCICE exam) — Full-day rate: $566
  - **Professionally Qualified** (other recognized credentials) — Full-day: $495
  - **Language Skilled** (no certification, less common languages) — Full-day: $350
- Courts contact interpreters through the **National Court Interpreter Database (NCID)**
- To be in NCID, individual interpreters register through their local federal court

**FCICE (Federal Court Interpreter Certification Exam):**
- Spanish/English ONLY
- Two-phase process: Written screener + Oral performance exam
- **2026 schedule:** Written phase registration opens **April 8, 2026**; exam administered **May 11–23, 2026**. Oral phase registration opens **June 22, 2026**; exam in **August 2026**
- Questions: **fcice@ao.uscourts.gov**

**How Catena gets business with federal courts:**
- Federal courts primarily hire *individual* certified interpreters, not agencies — but they do use agencies for hard-to-fill requests
- Contact the Clerk's Office at the US District Court for Colorado to inquire about getting your agency on their contact list
- For non-Spanish languages (Arabic, Somali, Vietnamese, Amharic, etc.), "language skilled" and "professionally qualified" tiers are common — this is where a multi-language agency has real value

**US Bankruptcy Court, District of Colorado** — separate court, also in Denver, also needs interpreters. Contact them independently.

### 1C. Denver Municipal Court

**Who they are:** City and County of Denver municipal court handling traffic, ordinance violations, misdemeanors. 

**Website:** **www.denvergov.org** (search "Municipal Court")

**Procurement:** Goes through the **City and County of Denver General Services — Purchasing Division**
- Denver uses its own procurement system
- Vendor registration: **denver.mwdbe.com** (also where Minority/Women-owned Business certifications are handled)
- The Purchasing Division hosts regular vendor outreach events — worth attending

**Small Business Certification:** Denver has a Small Business Enterprise (SBE) certification program. As a startup, Catena may qualify — this can give preference in city contracts. Register at **denver.mwdbe.com**.

**Key approach:** Call the Denver Municipal Court clerk's office directly and ask who manages their language services contract and when it renews. This is the fastest way to find out if they're in a contract window.

### General Hospital Vendor Strategy

1. **Get a capabilities statement ready:** 1-page document with languages covered, interpreter qualifications, response times, compliance (HIPAA, etc.), rates, and contact info
2. **Target Language Services Managers first** — they're the functional buyers even if Procurement writes the PO
3. **Offer pilot programs:** Offer 30-day supplemental coverage for their hardest-to-fill languages at competitive rates
4. **HIPAA BAA ready:** Hospitals will require a Business Associate Agreement before any patient contact — have this ready to sign
5. **Interpreter credentials:** Hospitals want to see CMI certification or at minimum CCHI/NBCMI credentials for medical interpreters

## PART 4: CERTIFICATIONS & REQUIREMENTS

### 4A. Colorado State Court Interpreter Certification

Colorado certifies court interpreters through the **Colorado Judicial Branch** using the **Consortium for State Court Interpreter Certification** testing program.

- **Languages tested:** Spanish, and others as available
- **Tiers:**
  - **Certified** — passed oral and written exams
  - **Registered** — passed written, pending oral or testing not available for their language
  - **Qualified** — assessed by the court for rare languages where formal testing doesn't exist
- **How to get certified:** Contact the Colorado Judicial Branch's Access to Justice Unit (via coloradojudicial.gov → Court Services → Access to Justice)
- For rare languages, courts accept professional credential review or use "qualified" status

> **For Catena as an agency:** Your interpreters' certification level determines what types of assignments they can handle. Certified interpreters command higher rates and can work high-stakes proceedings. Register your interpreter roster appropriately.

---

### 4B. Federal Court Interpreter Certification (FCICE)

- Spanish/English only
- Administered by the Administrative Office of US Courts
- **2026 exam windows:** Written May 11–23; Oral August 2026
- Register at: **prometric.com/exams/aousc**
- Questions: **fcice@ao.uscourts.gov**
- Without FCICE, interpreters can still work federal courts as "professionally qualified" (with other recognized credentials) or "language skilled" (for less common languages)

---

### 4C. Medical Interpreter Certification

Colorado does **not** mandate a specific medical interpreter certification, but hospitals strongly prefer certified interpreters. Two nationally recognized credentials:

**NBCMI — National Board of Certification for Medical Interpreters**
- **CMI (Certified Medical Interpreter)** credential
- Website: **certifiedmedicalinterpreters.org**
- Languages: Spanish, Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, Russian, Portuguese, Arabic, Japanese, and more
- Written + oral exam

**CCHI — Certification Commission for Healthcare Interpreters**
- **CoreCHI** (written knowledge only) and **CHI** (full certification with oral)
- Website: **healthcareinterpretercertification.org**
- Available in Spanish and other languages

**For Catena:** Having CMI- or CHI-certified interpreters on your roster is a significant competitive advantage when pitching hospitals. At minimum, all medical interpreters should be able to show completion of 40-hour medical interpreter training.

---

### 4D. No Colorado State License Required

Colorado does not currently require a business license specifically for interpretation companies. Standard requirements apply:
- Register your business with the Colorado Secretary of State (**sos.colorado.gov**)
- Obtain a Denver business license if operating in the city (**denvergov.org/licenses**)
- General liability insurance (typically $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate — hospitals and courts will require proof)
- Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance is recommended, especially for legal/medical
- HIPAA compliance program (required for any healthcare client)

## PART 5: PROFESSIONAL ASSOCIATIONS

### 5A. NAJIT — National Association of Judiciary Interpreters and Translators
- **Website:** najit.org
- **Membership:** Corporate — **$250/year** (name in the corporation, not individual)
- **Benefits:** Directory listing, job board, certification prep resources, networking, NAJIT logo on marketing materials, reduced-rate professional liability insurance access
- **Best for:** Court/legal interpretation credibility
- **Recommendation:** Join as a Corporate member — it signals legitimacy to courts and attorneys

### 5B. ATA — American Translators Association
- **Website:** atanet.org
- **Membership:** Corporate (revenue under $5M) — **$500/year**
- **Benefits:** Directory listing, networking, industry credibility
- **Best for:** Translation work, not interpretation specifically — consider if Catena plans to offer document translation

### 5C. NCIHC — National Council on Interpreting in Health Care
- **Website:** ncihc.org
- **Best for:** Healthcare interpretation community, standards, advocacy
- **Benefits:** Community discussion forums, publications, conference, standards documents your medical clients expect you to follow
- **Membership:** Individual and organizational levels available

### 5D. IMIA — International Medical Interpreters Association
- **Website:** imiaweb.org
- **Corporate/ISP (Interpreter Service Provider) division:** Has an ISP membership track
- **Benefits:** ISP directory listing, interpreter registry, job board, training notices
- **Best for:** Healthcare interpretation agency credibility

### 5E. Colorado-Specific Networking
- **Denver Metro Chamber of Commerce** — general business networking, connections to healthcare and legal communities
- **Colorado Hispanic Chamber of Commerce** — relevant given Spanish as primary language need
- **Rocky Mountain Minority Supplier Development Council** — if Catena qualifies for minority-owned business status
- **Denver Office of Economic Development** — Small Business Certification events (see denvergov.org/purchasing)

## PART 6: REALISTIC TIMELINE — ZERO TO FIRST CONTRACT

### Month 1: Foundation
- [ ] Register Catena on Colorado Secretary of State (if not done)
- [ ] Get general liability insurance and E&O insurance quotes
- [ ] Create HIPAA compliance policy and BAA template
- [ ] Register on BidNet for Colorado Judicial solicitations
- [ ] Join NAJIT as Corporate member ($250)
- [ ] Create capabilities statement (1-page PDF)
- [ ] Apply to subcontract with 3–5 agencies (LanguageLine, CyraCom, Propio, GLOBO, CLI)

### Month 2: Active Outreach
- [ ] Email FSDPurchasing@judicial.state.co.us to introduce Catena
- [ ] Contact Denver Municipal Court clerk — ask about language services contract and renewal date
- [ ] Cold-call Language Services Managers at Denver Health, UCHealth, Children's Colorado
- [ ] Register with Green Security (UCHealth requirement) if pursuing UCHealth
- [ ] Begin background checks for your interpreter roster (ADB/Complio)
- [ ] Attend a Denver City purchasing outreach event

### Month 3: Proposals & Pilots
- [ ] Submit response to any open BidNet solicitations relevant to interpretation
- [ ] Offer a free 30-day pilot to 1–2 hospitals for rare-language coverage
- [ ] Agency subcontract agreements should be signed by now — expect first assignments
- [ ] NCIHC membership (if doing healthcare)
- [ ] Follow up with federal court clerk's office about NCID registration for your interpreters

### Month 4–6: First Direct Contracts
- **Subcontract revenue:** Should be flowing by Month 2–3
- **State court or municipal court:** Realistic first direct contract by Month 4–6 if there's an open procurement window; otherwise you wait for next RFP
- **Hospital:** First direct contract typically takes 6–12 months (longer procurement cycles, committee approvals)
- **Federal court:** Individual interpreters can be in NCID by Month 3; agency-level relationship takes longer

### Month 6–12: Scale
- Build a track record of completed assignments
- Collect testimonials and reference letters
- Prepare for state court re-bid cycles (often 1–3 year contracts)
- Pursue Joint Commission accreditation (valued by hospitals, not required)
